Abbreviations & load guide
1RM
One-repetition maximum: the heaviest load you can lift once with sound technique.
e1RM
Estimated one-repetition maximum, calculated from a multi-repetition set.
RPE
Rating of perceived exertion on a 1-10 scale. RPE 8 means about two good reps remain; RPE 9 means one remains; RPE 10 is a true maximum.
RFD
Rate of force development: how quickly force is produced.
RFE
Rear-foot elevated.
BB / DB / SA
Barbell / dumbbell / single-arm.
ER / Iso
External rotation / isometric hold.
SR
Strength/resistance session.

How to calculate % e1RM

Use a recent, technically sound set of 3-8 reps for the same exercise. A set near RPE 9-10 gives the most useful estimate.

e1RM = load x (1 + reps / 30)

Example: 40 kg for 5 reps gives 40 x (1 + 5 / 30) = 46.7 kg. A 70% prescription is 46.7 x 0.70 = 32.7 kg, rounded to the nearest available load, usually 32.5 kg.

For split squats, use the total load held in both hands and the reps completed on the weaker side. Recalculate when a recent set changes. If the prescribed load exceeds the stated RPE or rep speed slows, reduce it by 2.5-5%; quality takes priority over the estimate.
